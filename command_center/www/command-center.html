Why these numbers probably understate the problem
  • Two jobs are recorded twice — the same X-ray setup, one spelled wrong. Counted as two, it is one.

  • A finding records what was found, the evidence, how serious it is and who owns fixing it.

Every individual change is kept, not a summary. One invoice edited four times appears four times, each with the field that changed, its value before and after, who made it and when. That is what makes it useful to an auditor rather than merely reassuring.
What this platform adds to it
  • Every sign-in, successful or refused, with the account and the time.
  • Every approval — who asked, who decided, what they decided, which records were attached, and any comment.
  • Every question put to the assistant, the records it read to answer, and any action it took on someone's behalf.
  • Every change to who can see what, and who made it.
  • Every threshold and rule change an administrator makes.
ERPNext already records what changed on a document. What it does not record is who looked, who approved, who was refused, and what was asked of the assistant. Those are added here and kept to the same standard — written once, with each entry carrying a fingerprint of the one before it, so removing a line from the database breaks the chain visibly.
